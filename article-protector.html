Mind

Becoming

October 2026 5 min read

There’s a word at the center of everything Aeilus is: becoming.

It’s in our tagline, For Men Becoming. It runs through almost everything I write here. And I chose it on purpose, because I believe it’s the most honest word there is for a man’s life. We are always in process. Life keeps happening, with new challenges, triumphs, and heartbreaks, and every one of those moments shapes us in some way.

But here’s the part that took me years to understand: becoming isn’t automatically good. We can become bitter or we can become loving. Resentful or cheerful. Harder or wiser. Every experience moves us somewhere. What decides the direction is choice.

This is how I learned that.

The man I used to be

I was picked on as a kid, and it didn’t stop when I became a teenager. Somewhere along the way, I shut down. I became wary of people, because people had taught me that letting them in usually meant getting hurt.

When the time came that I had to come out of my shell, I discovered a version of myself I didn’t know existed. He was strong. He was combative. And he had one job: keep people at a distance.

I call him the protector.

He took over anytime there was conflict or I felt disrespected. Often he didn’t even wait for anything to happen. He was always scanning for danger, and if he sensed it coming, he struck first. Not with his fists, but with words, with coldness, with a wall that went up before anyone could get close enough to hurt me again.

For a long time, I thought he was simply who I was.

The cost of protection

When I look back on my life now, there are people I think about and feel bad. I know I didn’t treat some of them well. If you asked them about me, they probably wouldn’t have a lot of good things to say.

That’s hard to sit with. But I’ve come to understand something important: I’m not that bad person, and I never was. The way I reacted came from a place of hurt. That doesn’t make it okay, and I won’t pretend it does. But identifying the root cause is what finally let me start doing better.

I think a lot of men are carrying this same weight. You look back at the way you’ve treated people, the relationships you burned, the moments you lashed out, and you decide that’s just who you are. Bad temper. Hard to love. Better off alone.

But what if that isn’t who you are? What if it’s who you had to become to survive what you went through?

The protector did his job

I want to be fair to the protector, because he deserves it. He did a great job for years. I needed to heal, and before I could, someone had to stand guard over the wounds so they wouldn’t be reopened. He stepped up.

I wouldn’t have the internal strength I have today without him.

That’s what gets missed in a lot of conversations about men, anger, and the walls we put up. Our defenses aren’t stupid. They made sense once. They kept us standing. The problem is that a protector built to keep out danger can’t tell the difference between someone who wants to hurt you and someone who wants to love you. So he keeps everyone out. And the same wall that kept you safe starts keeping you alone.

The change I noticed looking back

I wish I could tell you there was one dramatic moment when I decided to be different. There wasn’t. I noticed the change looking back.

Situations that used to set me off, like conflict, disrespect, or someone coming at me sideways, started landing differently. Instead of striking first, I found myself letting what was going to happen simply happen. Then, once it had, I’d thoughtfully choose the most appropriate response.

It felt good. Not good like winning an argument. Good like seeing growth I could actually see. I could measure it in the space between what happened and what I did about it.

Now that I’ve noticed that space, I’m conscious of it in every situation. That’s the real difference. The change happened slowly, mostly without my noticing. But now that I know the choice is there, I get to make it on purpose. And because I do, I’m at peace more often than not.

The protector, semi-retired

He’s still there. I don’t think he’ll ever be completely gone, and honestly, I don’t need him to be. He’s semi-retired now. He doesn’t run the show anymore. He doesn’t scan every room for threats or decide how I respond before I’ve had a chance to think. But he earned his place, and I’m grateful for him.

Becoming didn’t mean getting rid of who I was. It meant letting the part of me that was built for survival step back, so the part of me that wants to live fully could step forward.

Why Aeilus is for men becoming

This is why Aeilus exists, and why it’s built the way it is.

Aeilus isn’t for men who have it all figured out. It’s not for men chasing a finished, polished version of themselves. It’s for men in process: men noticing their own patterns for the first time, men looking back with regret and wondering if they can still change, men who built walls a long time ago and are starting to wonder what’s on the other side.

The five pillars, Mind, Body, Fuel, Knowledge, and Connection, are simply the places where that becoming happens. How you think. How you treat your body. What you put into it. What you learn. Who you let in. Each one is a daily choice about which direction you’re moving.

You are going to become something. Life guarantees that. The question is what.

Bitter or loving. Guarded or open. Stuck as the man your hurt made you, or choosing the man you want to be.

I’m still becoming. So are you. Let’s choose well.
